Spyware Awareness

Pegasus Spyware – Global Surveillance Awareness

What is Pegasus? Pegasus is a sophisticated commercial spyware product developed by NSO Group, an Israeli technology company. NSO Group states it sells Pegasus only to vetted government clients for law enforcement and intelligence purposes. However, investigations by multiple organisations have documented its alleged use against journalists, human rights defenders, lawyers and opposition politicians in various countries.

How it works (technical overview for awareness): Pegasus exploits vulnerabilities in mobile operating systems (iOS and Android) to gain deep access to a target device. It has used both "one-click" infection methods (requiring the target to click a link) and "zero-click" methods (requiring no action from the target — a WhatsApp call or iMessage was enough in documented cases). Once installed, Pegasus can theoretically access: messages (including encrypted apps), emails, photos, location data, microphone and camera.

Indian context: In 2021, a global investigative project (the "Pegasus Project") led by Forbidden Stories and Amnesty International, involving 17 media partners including The Wire (India), analysed a leaked list of over 50,000 phone numbers reportedly selected as potential surveillance targets. Indian phone numbers appeared on this list. The Government of India neither confirmed nor denied the use of Pegasus. The Supreme Court of India appointed a technical committee to investigate (2021). This is an ongoing and contested area — the following is factual reporting of what has been publicly documented.

  • Pegasus has been documented by Citizen Lab (University of Toronto) in detailed technical reports since 2016
  • Apple released emergency security updates in September 2021 after Citizen Lab discovered a zero-click iOS exploit used to deliver Pegasus
  • Apple now notifies users it believes may have been targeted by state-sponsored spyware
  • The case highlights that even fully updated devices can be vulnerable to sophisticated, well-funded state-level threats
  • WhatsApp filed a lawsuit against NSO Group in 2019 (US District Court, Northern California)
💡 Key Lessons for Everyday Users: State-level spyware like Pegasus is generally not a threat to ordinary individuals — it is expensive and reportedly targeted. However, it demonstrates why security updates matter (every Pegasus infection depended on unpatched vulnerabilities) and why device hygiene is important. For most people: keep your OS and apps updated, use end-to-end encrypted messaging apps (Signal, WhatsApp), enable MFA on accounts, and be cautious about clicking links in messages from unknown senders. If you are a journalist or human rights worker handling sensitive information, consult resources from Amnesty Tech or Citizen Lab.
