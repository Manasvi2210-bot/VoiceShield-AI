📋 Common Digital Payment Fraud Types Know Your Risks
📞

Suspicious Bank / Authority Calls

Fraudsters impersonate bank staff, RBI officials, income-tax officers or police. They create fear ("your account will be frozen") or urgency. Real bank staff never ask for your OTP, PIN, CVV or password over the phone. Always hang up and call the official number printed on your card or the bank's official website.

🔑

OTP & CVV Requests

OTP (One-Time Password) is your second layer of security. Banks generate OTPs for you to enter into their own app — they never need to ask you for it. Similarly, your CVV is printed on your card for you to enter on payment pages — no one should ask you to share it verbally or via message.

📱

UPI Scams

Common UPI scams: Collect requests — scammer sends a UPI collect request (which debits YOUR account) while telling you they are "sending" money; Fake QR codes — scanning a QR code can initiate a payment from your account; PIN sharing — you only need your UPI PIN to pay, never to receive. Receiving money requires NO PIN.

📸

Fake Payment Confirmations

Fraudsters send a screenshot or edited image showing a fake payment confirmation and claim they have paid. Always verify credits in your official bank app or bank statement — never rely on screenshots or messages from the other party. This scam is common in second-hand goods transactions (OLX, Quikr).

🔗

Suspicious Payment Links

Links sent via SMS, WhatsApp or email that appear to be payment portals but are phishing sites designed to steal credentials. Signs: unusual domain names, no HTTPS, request for OTP or PIN on an external page, not matching the official bank/payment app URL. Always go directly to the official website by typing the URL.

📦

QR Code Scams

Scammers send QR codes claiming they are for receiving money, but scanning and entering your PIN actually sends money to the scammer. QR codes on physical surfaces (parking, restaurants) can also be replaced with malicious ones. Always verify the UPI ID before completing any transaction.

✅ Safe Digital Payment Practices Best Practices

🏦 Banking Safety

  • Enable transaction alerts (SMS + email) for all accounts.
  • Set a daily/per-transaction limit on UPI and internet banking.
  • Use only RBI-regulated payment apps (PhonePe, Google Pay, BHIM, Paytm).
  • Never share your UPI PIN, card PIN, CVV or OTP with anyone.
  • Verify IFSC and account number twice before transferring money.
  • Immediately report unauthorised transactions to your bank (usually within 3 days for liability protection).

🛒 E-Commerce Safety

  • Shop only on HTTPS websites with a padlock icon.
  • Check seller ratings and reviews on established platforms.
  • Avoid paying by direct bank transfer to unknown sellers.
  • Use credit cards when possible — they offer chargeback protection.
  • Keep order confirmation emails and payment receipts as evidence.
  • Verify the exact domain — "flipkart-sale.com" is not Flipkart.
🏦 RBI Customer Protection: RBI's circular on "Limiting Liability of Customers in Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions" (RBI/2017-18/15, July 2017) states that customers may have zero liability for unauthorised transactions if they are not at fault and report promptly. Contact your bank and visit rbi.org.in for official guidelines. For disputes, you may approach the RBI Banking Ombudsman at rbi.org.in.

Note: This is introductory information. For specific legal advice regarding your situation, consult your bank or a financial legal expert.
📖 Case Study Real Incident
🏦
Banking Cyberattack

Cosmos Cooperative Bank Cyberattack (August 2018)

What happened: In August 2018, Cosmos Cooperative Bank — one of India's oldest cooperative banks, headquartered in Pune, Maharashtra — suffered a significant cyberattack. Hackers compromised the bank's ATM server infrastructure and stole approximately ₹94.42 crore (approximately USD 13.5 million). The attack took place in two phases over two weekends in August 2018.

How it worked: Attackers installed malware on the bank's ATM switch server (the system that approves or declines ATM transactions). This malware created a parallel, fraudulent payment switch. When the fraudulent cloned debit cards were used at ATMs in 28 countries simultaneously, the legitimate switch was bypassed and the malware-controlled fraudulent switch approved all transactions regardless of actual account balances. In a separate but related incident, approximately ₹13.92 crore was transferred via SWIFT (international wire transfer) to a Hong Kong-based company account. Pune Police Cyber Cell filed an FIR and investigated the case.

  • Phase 1 (11 August 2018): ~₹78 crore swiped using cloned Visa cards at 12,000 transactions across 28 countries in ~2 hours
  • Phase 2 (13 August 2018): ~₹2.5 crore via Rupay card transactions in India
  • ₹13.92 crore transferred internationally via SWIFT to Hong Kong
  • Attack attributed to Lazarus Group (North Korea-linked) by some cybersecurity researchers, though official attribution varies by source
  • Highlighted vulnerabilities in India's cooperative bank infrastructure and ATM switch security
💡 Key Lessons: This attack targeted bank infrastructure, not individual users. Banks must implement robust endpoint security on ATM servers, monitor for anomalous transaction patterns in real-time and segment payment switch networks from general IT infrastructure. For individuals: enable transaction alerts so you are immediately notified of any unauthorised activity, even small amounts. Report unknown transactions to your bank immediately — early reporting is critical. This case also demonstrates why SWIFT-based international transfers require strong authentication controls.
